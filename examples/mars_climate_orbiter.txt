NASA’s Mars orbiter came in about 170 kilometers lower than planned.

A software file helped send it there.

The Mars Climate Orbiter lost contact during its attempt to enter orbit in 1999.

The investigation found a units mismatch in a ground-software file—not aboard the spacecraft. The file reported thruster impulses in pound-force seconds. The navigation system expected newton-seconds.

That mismatch made the navigation model underestimate how much the thrusters changed the spacecraft’s path.

The investigation also found that correction maneuver number five was not performed, along with gaps in communication and software checks.

The failure wasn’t just the wrong units. It was a system that failed to catch them before the spacecraft was lost.
