At 2:17 a.m., Daniel was alone in the hospital switchboard when a dead extension rang. The number belonged to a ward disconnected eleven years earlier—the night his sister died. He answered. No voice spoke, only breathing, then three words: Don't send the elevator. The call ended. The switchboard printed tomorrow's emergency log, signed with Daniel's name. On the fourth floor, one elevator button was warm. Beneath it, a red light pulsed behind a sealed records door. Daniel had filed his sister's death as an accident. Now the call replayed the minute he refused to remember. In the original log, he found the rule: every answered call demanded a witness. Destroy the page, and the past stayed buried. Route it, and someone tomorrow might live. Daniel connected the line. The hospital went dark. The receiver rang from inside the sealed room. At dawn, the new log held one completed call: Daniel's name—followed by home.
