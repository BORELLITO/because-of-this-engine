At six past one a.m., Mara, a city archivist, digitized the last security tape from a condemned train station.

The recording ended at fourteen past two. Then the file added one more minute.

In that extra minute, every platform clock stopped at seven past one. A maintenance door opened by itself.

Mara checked the blueprint. The door had been bricked shut after a worker disappeared there in 1989.

On the tape, the missing worker crossed the platform carrying a lamp. He stopped beneath the camera and looked directly into the lens.

Mara froze the frame. Behind him, a second shadow stood where nobody was standing.

Then the timestamp jumped to the present. The station's live monitor showed that platform—empty, except for a lamp glowing behind the sealed wall.

Police found no broken door, footprints, or extra minute on the original tape.

Mara's copy changed again, ending with a new frame: someone on the platform holding the camera from the other side.
