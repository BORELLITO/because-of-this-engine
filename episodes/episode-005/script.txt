At 2:14 a.m., the elevator in Saint Arden Hospital stopped at Floor 13.

The problem was simple: Saint Arden had only twelve floors.

Night guard Elias checked the panel. There was no button for thirteen, no basement access, and no reason for the elevator to stop. But the system log showed the doors had remained open for forty-seven seconds.

He rewound the lobby camera.

The doors opened. Beyond them was a narrow concrete corridor, lit by a single red emergency lamp. Elias watched himself step inside—then the footage cut to black.

Maintenance found nothing. The shaft had no thirteenth landing. They replaced the control board and locked the elevator overnight.

At 2:14 the next morning, it stopped again.

This time, the camera stayed on.

The doors opened to the same corridor. At the far end stood Elias, wearing the uniform he had worn the night before.

He raised one hand and pointed at the floor.

When the doors closed, the log recorded a second passenger.

Elias was still standing in the lobby.

And the elevator began descending below the basement.
